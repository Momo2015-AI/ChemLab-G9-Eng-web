## Passed / acceptable

- The lesson distinguishes HCl from hydrochloric acid as an aqueous solution.
- Concentrated hydrochloric acid is presented as volatile.
- Concentrated sulfuric acid is presented as strongly water-absorbing and corrosive.
- The lesson avoids giving students a procedural recipe for hazardous concentrated-acid handling and routes safety to teacher/laboratory rules.
- The rust-removal lesson separates observation from interpretation and explicitly warns that solution color alone is not sufficient proof of a specific ion.
- The rust model is framed as a junior-high representation rather than claiming rust is pure Fe2O3.
- The lesson correctly warns against the unconditional rule “all acids react with all metals to produce hydrogen”.

## Issues requiring resolution

### P1 — HCl terminology remains context-sensitive

The lesson correctly explains that HCl denotes hydrogen chloride and hydrochloric acid is its aqueous solution. However, the worked example still uses a classification question whose wording can make `HCl` appear to be an unqualified name for the acid in a school-context item. The item should be rewritten so the state/context is explicit and the learner is not forced to resolve a terminology ambiguity unrelated to the intended learning objective.

### P1 — Rust model needs controlled wording everywhere

The lesson already uses “Fe2O3 as a junior-high model”. This wording must be preserved in any future question, explanation, or experiment record. Do not silently normalize “rust = Fe2O3” as an absolute statement.

### P1 — Experiment observation should distinguish expected from conditional observations

“Solution may appear yellow” and “bubbles may appear after rust removal” are context-dependent observations. Assessment items must not treat either as a universal guaranteed observation. The experiment content should use “可能/在条件满足时” language consistently.

### P1 — Diagnostic safety item should assess reasoning, not operational procedure

The diagnostic should test recognition of the heat/splash hazard and the need to follow approved supervision, rather than asking students to reproduce detailed hazardous handling steps.

### P2 — Cognitive load

The scientific content is appropriate for Grade 9, but the original rendering exposed too much continuous text. The V2.1 UI redesign addresses this presentation issue by grouping content into visual modules and keeping the learner's next action visible.

## Release gate

Day 01 remains blocked until the P1 terminology/item issue is rewritten and the final scientific + age-suitability review is signed off.
