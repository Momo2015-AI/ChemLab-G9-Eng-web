# V1.9 Content Audit — Final Gate Definition

## Objective

Make content quality a release gate rather than a manual checklist.

## Five audit layers

### 1. Source provenance
Every production item must identify its content provenance and review state. National curriculum and adopted textbook material are the primary curriculum anchors; teacher-authored/original questions require scientific review before publication.

### 2. Scientific correctness
Check terminology, formulas, equations, conditions, observations, causal explanations, experimental assumptions, and safety framing. Ambiguous experimental premises are publication blockers.

### 3. Grade-9 suitability
Content must stay within the intended junior-high chemistry scope unless explicitly marked as extension material. Cognitive demand must match the stated Bloom level and difficulty.

### 4. Question quality
A single-answer item must have exactly one defensible answer. Distractors must be plausible, the explanation must prove the key, and no hidden assumption may change the answer.

### 5. Knowledge relationships
Every question's knowledge IDs must resolve to canonical graph nodes. Graph references to nonexistent questions are errors. Question → knowledge → experiment/misconception/remediation relationships should be auditable.

## Release rule

`ready` is a release state, not a drafting state. Any P0 issue, unresolved broken reference, malformed question, missing explanation, or invalid taxonomy field blocks the content integrity workflow.

## Important limitation

The automated gate can prove structural integrity and enforce known review decisions; it cannot replace a qualified human scientific review of every question. The 320-question bank therefore remains subject to item-level scientific sign-off even after CI is green.
